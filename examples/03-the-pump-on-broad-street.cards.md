# Knowledge cards: The Pump on Broad Street

Source: examples/03-the-pump-on-broad-street.input.md | Cards: 7

---

## Card 1: Miasma, the theory Snow argued against
- **Core knowledge**: The dominant theory of cholera in 1854 was miasma: the disease travelled through the air, carried by foul smells rising from the city's filth.
- **Explanation**: Nobody knew what caused cholera at the time, and miasma was the theory of the day. Snow was a London physician who had spent years studying cholera, and he did not believe in miasma.
- **Example or self-test**: Self-test: According to miasma, how did cholera travel? Answer: through the air, carried by foul smells rising from the city's filth.

---

## Card 2: Snow's waterborne suspicion came from earlier outbreaks
- **Core knowledge**: In earlier outbreaks, Snow had noticed that cholera seemed to follow the water supply rather than the air.
- **Explanation**: That earlier observation is why he did not believe in miasma. So when the Soho outbreak began, he started walking the streets with a map.
- **Example or self-test**: Self-test: Why did Snow begin the Soho outbreak by walking the streets with a map? Answer: because he had already noticed that cholera seemed to follow the water supply rather than the air.

---

## Card 3: Marks on a map revealed a shape the numbers alone had not shown
- **Core knowledge**: Because Snow drew the deaths as marks on a map instead of writing them as a list, the shape of the outbreak became visible at a glance: the deaths clustered around the water pump on Broad Street.
- **Explanation**: He marked every death with a short black bar at the address where the person had died, so the deaths accumulated on the map rather than as a list of numbers. Only when enough bars had accumulated did a pattern emerge.
- **Example or self-test**: Source instance: the map showed that some blocks near the pump had lost almost every household.

---

## Card 4: Places that opted out of the predicted exposure are not counterexamples
- **Core knowledge**: A place that escaped the exposure a theory says matters was not an exception that weakened the theory, but a place that had opted out of that exposure.
- **Explanation**: Snow used both of the facts that complicated the story, rather than setting them aside. The workhouse a few streets away had suffered few deaths, and it too had its own water source.
- **Example or self-test**: Source instance: the nearby brewery lost almost none of its workers because it had its own well and its employees were given beer as part of their wages, so they drank little water from the pump.

---

## Card 5: Removing the handle did not itself stop the deaths
- **Core knowledge**: The pump handle was taken off on 8 September, but the outbreak was already fading by then, which is why some historians argue that removing the handle did not itself stop the deaths.
- **Explanation**: The epidemic had largely burned through the susceptible population before the handle came off. The removal is remembered anyway, because it marked the moment a public authority acted on the waterborne theory.
- **Example or self-test**: Self-test: Why do some historians argue that removing the handle did not itself stop the deaths? Answer: because the outbreak was already fading, as the epidemic had largely burned through the susceptible population.

---

## Card 6: What Snow built was a method, not a proof
- **Core knowledge**: What Snow had was not a proof but a method: plot the cases by location, look for the shape, and then test the shape against the places that should not fit.
- **Explanation**: He could not yet point to the organism that caused cholera; that would come decades later. The source argues that the more transferable part of Broad Street is this move that came before the drawing and after it — having a hypothesis, looking for an arrangement of the evidence that would reveal whether the hypothesis was right, and deliberately going looking for the cases that disagreed with him — rather than the map's reputation in data visualization.
- **Example or self-test**: Self-test: When testing his hypothesis, what did Snow deliberately go looking for? Answer: the cases that disagreed with him.

---

## Card 7: Comparing the two water companies scaled the argument to cause
- **Core knowledge**: When Snow obtained the water companies' records, districts supplied by the contaminated intake had far higher cholera death rates than districts supplied by the other company.
- **Explanation**: He used the same logic on a much larger scale, comparing cholera deaths between districts served by two different companies drawing from different stretches of the Thames. That comparison turned a neighbourhood pattern into an argument about cause.
- **Example or self-test**: Self-test: What did Snow compare to test the waterborne theory beyond a single neighbourhood? Answer: cholera deaths between districts served by two companies drawing from different stretches of the Thames.
